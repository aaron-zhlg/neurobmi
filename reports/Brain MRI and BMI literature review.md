# Regional morphometry explains only six percent of BMI

On HCP-YA, the closest published match to a FreeSurfer-region linear model predicts BMI poorly. Xu et al. (2023) trained elastic net on regional thickness, surface area and subcortical volume in 1,100 HCP-YA adults and reached a **held-out R² of 6.35% from brain features alone**. The 8.87% in their abstract comes only after adding demographics ([Xu 2023, PMC12614112](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12614112/)). Obese-vs-lean classification from the same kind of features gives **AUC 0.648** in HCP-YA ([Xu 2026, PubMed 42581075](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42581075/)) and **AUC 0.687** in the 6,420-person ENIGMA-MDD mega-analysis, which drops to 59.5% balanced accuracy under leave-one-site-out ([Opel 2021, PMC8589644](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8589644/)). The mass-univariate picture fits these modest numbers. In large samples, obesity goes with diffuse cortical thinning of Cohen's d ≈ −0.2 to −0.33, strongest in fusiform and temporal cortex. Surface-area effects are tiny and mixed in sign, and some subcortical volumes (amygdala, thalamus, accumbens) are *larger* in obesity. In HCP-YA, whose participants are young and span a narrow age range, even these effects shrink, and one family-aware mixed model on 897 participants found **no BMI association with cortical thickness or surface area at all** ([Medic 2019, PMC6462878](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6462878/)). Much larger figures appear in the literature: r = 0.44–0.70 from HCP connectome models, and R² = 0.33–0.46 from 3D-CNNs on whole T1 volumes. They come from different feature types, and the connectome numbers are exposed to head motion, the strongest known non-neural correlate of BMI. For a ridge baseline with 225 Desikan-Killiany features, family-grouped CV and fold-wise age/sex/ICV residualisation, a correct pipeline should therefore give **R² of about 0.03–0.08 (r ≈ 0.2–0.3) and AUC of about 0.60–0.68**. A result well above that range is more likely leakage than discovery.

## Twelve papers define the HCP-YA and large-cohort benchmark set

HCP-YA's BMI distribution is reported consistently across papers: mean **≈26.5 ± 5.2 kg/m²**, range about 16.5–47.8, with **about 22% (242–243 of ~1,100) at BMI ≥ 30** ([Brown 2023, PMC10509524](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10509524/); [Bachmann 2025, PMC11899596](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11899596/)). That stability makes cross-paper comparison easier. A 1,040-subject sample should therefore contain roughly 225–235 obese participants and about 450 normal-weight participants. Two things stand out in this set. First, roughly three-quarters of the HCP-YA BMI literature is mass-univariate (VBM, ROI regression, SurfStat, seed FC). **Only Bachmann 2025 explicitly reports family-aware cross-validation.** The abstracts and full text of the two closest structural ML benchmarks (Xu 2023, Xu 2026) do not say whether folds were grouped by family. HCP-YA consists mainly of twin and sibling families, so ungrouped folds let relatives predict each other, and those reported numbers could be optimistic. Second, **no HCP-YA BMI study I found uses PALM-style permutation with family exchangeability blocks**, which is the standard HCP approach to family-aware inference. A new baseline can fill both gaps at little cost.

## Thinning is real but small, and its map shifts with age

The best-powered FreeSurfer evidence comes from ENIGMA-MDD, which used the same Desikan-Killiany pipeline as most HCP work. Obese participants had mean hemispheric thinning of **d ≈ −0.21**. The largest regional effects were **bilateral fusiform (d ≈ −0.33)** and superior, inferior and middle temporal cortex (d ≈ −0.23 to −0.25), with about 50 regions significant, mostly temporal and frontal. Surface area behaved differently. Isthmus cingulate and paracentral area were *larger* (d up to +0.17), while rostral middle frontal and inferior temporal area were smaller (d ≈ −0.1). Amygdala (+0.21), thalamus (+0.13 to +0.15) and accumbens (+0.12) volumes were *larger* in obesity ([Opel 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8589644/)). Because thickness and area diverge, gray-matter volume, which combines both, sits in between and depends on which component dominates in a region. The 3D-CNN study on UK Biobank independently found no significant cortical-thickness association after correction and only an isthmus-cingulate area cluster ([Vakli 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7104804)). Isthmus cingulate area therefore replicates as a positive-direction marker.

VBM meta-analyses converge on lower gray matter in **medial prefrontal/orbitofrontal cortex, cerebellum and left temporal pole**. García-García (21 studies, N = 5,882) validated these clusters in 378 HCP participants ([García-García 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41366-018-0164-4)). Chen's ALE analysis (N = 7,612) found only one convergent cluster, in right OFC (BA10/11), and weaker effects in samples with mean age ≥ 40 ([Chen 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7509458)). In children, ABCD shows BMI-related thinning concentrated in lateral OFC, pars orbitalis/triangularis, rostral middle and superior frontal cortex, entorhinal cortex and temporal pole, with Desikan-Killiany features and motion, brain volume and SES all covaried ([Ronan 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7175011/); [Laurent 2020](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/article-abstract/2756127)). UK Biobank, in 45–76-year-olds, shows lower total and subcortical GMV with adiposity, more strongly for waist-hip ratio than BMI and mainly in men ([Hamer & Batty 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30626649/); [RSNA on Dekkers 2019](https://www.rsna.org/news/2019/april/obesity-linked-structure-brain)).

HCP-YA itself is less clear-cut. Studies agree that temporal pole and entorhinal/parahippocampal cortex thin with BMI (Park 2021, r = −0.21; Vainik 2018; Xu 2025 for entorhinal area), but they disagree elsewhere. Vainik reports thinner right frontal but *thicker* left lateral prefrontal and parieto-occipital cortex ([Vainik 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30154161/)). Morys reports *positive* dorsal frontal and occipital thickness associations in adult samples ([Morys 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11133431/)). Medic finds nothing at the global or lobar level ([Medic 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6462878/)). The most likely explanation is age. ENIGMA's thinning grows with age, with steeper age-related frontal and orbitofrontal thinning in obese participants and stronger effects in males ([Opel 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8589644/)), so a 22–35-year-old cohort sits at the weak end of the curve. A further reason concerns head size and causal timing. A life-course Mendelian randomization in 37,501 UK Biobank participants attributes many adult obesity–brain associations to *childhood* adiposity and the persistently larger head size that comes with it, rather than to midlife obesity ([Chiesa 2024, UK Biobank record](https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk/publications/childhood-adiposity-underlies-numerous-adult-brain-traits-commonly-attributed-to-midlife-obesity/)). Whether ICV is regressed out therefore changes the answer to the scientific question, not just the size of the effect. Twin work adds a third reason. In HCP twins, **77–89% of the BMI–brain phenotypic correlation is genetic** ([Vainik 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30154161/)). Yet BMI-discordant monozygotic co-twins still differ in prefrontal, striatal and cerebellar GMV ([Weise 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31299367/)), so some non-genetic component remains.

Subcortical results depend on age and on how ICV is handled. In HCP-YA, hypothalamic volume is *positively* related to BMI, though it explains only about 1% of variance (r² = 0.009–0.012) ([Brown 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10509524/)). Males with obesity have larger right amygdala volume ([He 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41656321/)), in line with ENIGMA's positive amygdala and thalamus effects. Older UK Biobank samples show the opposite, with smaller striatum and pallidum. A ridge model on the 21 aseg volumes should therefore load positively on amygdala, thalamus and accumbens, and these signs should not be mistaken for errors.

## Connectivity models score higher but carry a motion confound

Resting-state reviews of mostly small case-control studies describe a consistent pattern in obesity. DMN and executive-control connectivity are weaker, salience connectivity is stronger, OFC coupling is up and insula coupling is down, and hypothalamic coupling moves toward reward regions and away from cognitive ones ([Syan 2021](https://www.pubmed.gov/34425125/); [Parsons 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11154-021-09665-x)). The HCP-YA association studies fit this pattern in broad outline. Higher BMI goes with more differentiated unimodal–transmodal gradients ([Park 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8203627/)), dynamics shifted toward a sensorimotor metastate ([Han 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38269472/)), and greater FC variability in OFC and limbic networks, with partial r ≈ 0.12–0.18 after covarying mean FD ([Guo 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10300418/)).

Prediction accuracy depends strongly on how motion is handled. Siegel et al. found that in HCP, **BMI correlated with framewise displacement at r = 0.66**, the highest of 23 motion-related measures. The notes flag this figure for verification against the PDF because it is unusually high. Aggressive cleaning removed nearly all FC–behaviour relations seen with minimal cleaning ([Siegel 2017, PMC6410500](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6410500)). Head motion is heritable in HCP (h² = 0.43) and shares genetic influences with BMI ([Hodgson 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27744290/)). In Park 2021, adding motion control changed BMI–gradient effects by **+42% in most networks and −14% in frontoparietal**, so motion can either inflate or mask effects ([Park 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8203627/)). The published accuracies line up with motion handling. Bachmann's CPM reached r = 0.44 at rest and **0.70 for task-general FC**, but did not regress motion ([Bachmann 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11899596/)). Wang's RVR covaried mean FD, excluded high-motion HCP subjects and still reached r = 0.39 ([Wang 2023](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/33/13/8368/7109831)). A UK Biobank model transferred to HCP fell to **r = 0.146**, and its overall held-out r was 0.102 ([Park 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7643372)). Older-adult evidence shows that some FC effects are real. Beyer's posterior-DMN reduction held in a motion-matched subsample and replicated ([Beyer 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28397392/)). A within-cohort r of about 0.4 is therefore plausible for motion-controlled FC. Cross-cohort generalisation is closer to r = 0.1–0.15, and the 0.70 should be treated as an upper bound.

Structural features are less exposed but not immune. None of the structural HCP-YA BMI papers reviewed reports a T1 motion or image-quality covariate. In practice that is the main remaining structural analogue of the FC motion problem.

## Voxel-level and multimodal models set a higher ceiling

Atlas-ROI linear models sit at the bottom of the BMI prediction range. In the same HCP-YA cohort, a **3D-CNN on whole T1 volumes reached R² = 0.325** and, according to its authors, "massively outperformed" tabular ROI models (elastic net, random forest, TabNet), although the comparator numbers could not be retrieved ([Cooper 2025, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.11.648433v1.full); [published version](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S3050642525000405)). In UK Biobank, a 3D-CNN with N ≈ 18k reached r = 0.70 / R² = 0.46, with almost no change when age and sex were removed as inputs. It fell to R² = 0.21 after transfer to IXI ([Vakli 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7104804)). In ABCD children, elastic net on sMRI plus DTI gave test R² = 0.27 and rs-FC gave 0.14 ([Adise 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1878929321000396)). That figure comes from a larger, developmentally heterogeneous, multi-site sample and should not be pooled with adult HCP numbers. The vertex-wise "morphometricity" framework applied to HCP (n = 1,110) and UK Biobank finds that body-size traits account for a substantial share of grey-matter variance ([Couvy-Duchesne 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32687259/)). The signal exists at fine spatial scale, and averaging it into 68 parcels discards much of it.

Two caveats limit how far these numbers can be taken. The CNN gains are at least partly real, since Cooper and Xu share a lab and a cohort. Still, no high-performing study combines repeated nested, family-grouped CV with explicit image-quality control, and the evidence base on external validation shows that internal and external performance often diverge ([Rosenblatt 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39085406/)). Within Xu 2023 itself, the "neuroanatomical profile" score used for mediation was scored in-sample for training participants, which inflates downstream associations ([Xu 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12614112/)). Any derived brain-BMI score should be strictly out-of-fold.

## What the neurobmi baseline should expect and report

**Expected performance.** The ridge model uses 225 Desikan-Killiany features with age, sex and ICV regressed out within training folds, and family-grouped 5-fold CV. Xu's brain-only 6.35% is the anchor, but three differences should push the result *lower*. Xu used TIV and brainstem volume as features and did not report family grouping. The neurobmi model removes ICV, which carries some BMI signal through head size (the Chiesa mechanism). And with fewer samples per fold, 1,040 participants is a slightly harder setting than 1,100. A realistic range is **out-of-sample R² ≈ 0.02–0.07, Pearson r ≈ 0.15–0.28, MAE of about 3.8–4.2 kg/m²** against an SD of about 5.2. The MAE figure is my inference from the SD and the R², not a published value. For logistic regression on obese (≥30) vs normal (18.5–24.9), roughly 230 vs 470 participants, expect **AUC ≈ 0.60–0.67**, bracketed by Xu 2026's 0.648 (SVM, same cohort) and ENIGMA's leave-site-out 59.5% balanced accuracy. Because the obese/normal ratio is about 1:2, balanced accuracy is the right accompaniment to AUC. If R² comes out above about 0.12 or AUC above about 0.72, audit for leakage before celebrating. Likely causes are residualisation fitted on all data, a family split across folds, standardisation outside the fold, or ICV entering as a feature.

**Comparisons to replicate.** The most informative replication is Xu 2023's three-model design on the same data: a demographics-only model (age, sex, plus income/education if available; Xu got 2.6% held-out), brain-only, and brain + demographics. That design answers whether 225 regional features add anything beyond what age and sex already predict. Run the model twice, once with ICV regressed out and once with ICV (and brainstem) as a feature, so that the result can be placed against both Xu's specification and the head-size concern. Run thickness-only, area-only, volume-only and subcortical-only sub-models. The ENIGMA pattern predicts that thickness and subcortical volume carry most of the signal and that area is weak and sign-mixed. Check the univariate maps against published direction. Expect negative fusiform, temporal and temporal-pole thickness, negative entorhinal area, and positive amygdala, thalamus and accumbens volume ([Opel 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8589644/); [Xu 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39695278/)). Agreement here is a sanity check that the pipeline is wired correctly. Finally, test BMI×sex, since effects are stronger in males in both ENIGMA and HCP brain-age work ([Chin Fatt 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33254060/)).

**Confounds and reporting practices to add.** Five additions would make the baseline stronger than any published HCP-YA structural BMI model. First, use **nested, family-grouped inner CV** for the ridge alpha and the logistic C, and repeat the outer 5-fold split at least 10 times with different seeds, reporting mean ± SD, since a single 5-fold split on 1,040 participants has noticeable variance. Second, run **family-respecting permutation tests**, shuffling BMI between whole families rather than individuals, following Bachmann's within-test permutation precedent ([Bachmann 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11899596/)). Third, report **sum-of-squares R² alongside r and MAE**, because a model with r = 0.25 can still have near-zero or negative R² if its predictions are miscalibrated. Fourth, add **education/income and FreeSurfer image-quality metrics** as a sensitivity covariate set. BMI correlates with education (r = −0.165), fluid intelligence (r = −0.17) and blood pressure (r = 0.45) in HCP-YA ([Guo 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10300418/); [Park 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8203627/)). Removing them answers a different question, so report results both with and without them. Image quality is the structural analogue of the FC motion confound, and no prior paper controls it. Fifth, given BMI's right skew (range up to about 48), compare raw against **log-BMI** targets. A single 1.4% underweight tail also matters for the obese/normal classifier definition. When connectivity features are added later, mean FD has to be treated as a first-class confound, with FD residualised from features within folds, a QC-FC check, and a motion-matched sensitivity subsample ([Wang 2023](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/33/13/8368/7109831); [Beyer 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28397392/)).

## Conclusion

The literature suggests that an honest FreeSurfer-ROI BMI baseline on HCP-YA will look underwhelming, and that this is a useful result. A brain-only R² of a few percent fits a field in which univariate effects are d ≈ 0.2, mostly genetic, partly fixed in childhood, and weakest in young adults. The headline numbers that look much better come from feature sets that retain more information (voxels, edges) or from pipelines that have not ruled out motion and family leakage. The baseline's value lies less in its accuracy than in being the first HCP-YA structural BMI model with documented family-grouped nested CV, family-aware permutation inference, fold-wise confound removal and an explicit demographics-only comparator. It then becomes the reference against which any gain from CNNs or connectivity can be measured.

Two questions remain open and are worth answering. The first is how much of Xu 2023's 6.35% survives family grouping and ICV removal. The neurobmi pipeline can measure this directly by toggling each choice. The second is whether the HCP BMI variable is measured or self-reported, which one paper asserts and the notes could not confirm ([Guo 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10300418/)). Checking the HCP data dictionary would settle it, and self-reported BMI would add noise that further caps achievable R². Also unverified are the exact comparator numbers in Cooper 2025 and whether Xu 2023/2026 grouped folds by family. These are the first sources to read in full before writing up results.
